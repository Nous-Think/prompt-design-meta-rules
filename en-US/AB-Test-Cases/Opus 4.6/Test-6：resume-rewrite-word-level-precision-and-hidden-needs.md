## Input Correction

The fundamental problem with your current version is that **every bullet describes "duties" rather than "achievements."** "Responsible for client social media management" tells the interviewer your job scope but doesn't answer what they really want to know: "So how'd it go?" Your actual numbers and cases are actually very strong — the problem isn't insufficient material, but rather the original version's framework locks you into an "executor" register — and for your goal of jumping to brand-side marketing manager, this is fatal, because brand-side looks for **strategic ownership and the causal chain to business results**, not how many clients you've served.

Another hidden issue: you're jumping from agency to brand-side. The interviewer's default concern is "this person is used to serving multiple clients simultaneously with fast turnarounds — can they settle down and do long-term strategy for a single brand?" — so your experience must deliberately highlight the **deep brand-building cases** you've done (綠田 transformation, MATZ rebrand), rather than just emphasizing the efficiency of juggling many accounts.

## Core Decision

The rewrite strategy is based on three judgments:

1. **Restructure as "scale overview + categorized achievements"**: Open with one sentence establishing your operating scale (budget, brand count, team), instantly anchoring the interviewer on your caliber; every subsequent bullet follows a "challenge/action → quantified result" CAR structure.

2. **Order by brand-side manager value weights**: Brand transformation strategy > brand rebrand > social growth > ad performance > team management. The first two directly prove strategic leadership and brand thinking — the capabilities an agency person most needs to prove when moving brand-side.

3. **Language shift from "service provider" to "driver"**: All verbs switch to "led," "drove," "built," "architected" — delete weak verbs like "responsible for," "assisted," "participated"; simultaneously calculate percentage gains and include them, because the dual anchoring of absolute numbers plus relative change rates is what lets HR and hiring managers unfamiliar with your industry intuitively grasp the magnitude.

### Systematic Differences in Word Choice

Comparing both rewrites line by line, the following systematic patterns emerge:

**Pattern 1: Verb Energy Level**

| Position | A's verb | B's verb | Difference |
|----------|----------|----------|-----------|
| Opening | Coordinated simultaneously | Drove | B deletes redundant "simultaneously" (managing 4–6 brands inherently implies simultaneous) |
| Opening | (absent) | Managing... with a team of | B embeds budget management and team leadership in the opening; A defers team mention to the end |
| MATZ | Led → responsible for | Drove → led → architected | A slides from "led" back to "responsible for" (weak verb regression) within the same bullet; B maintains strong verbs throughout without repetition |

**Pattern characteristic**: A's verbs occasionally reach high energy (e.g., "led") in individual bullets but don't sustain — they regress to weak verbs ("responsible for") within the same bullet. B's verbs maintain "driver" register throughout without repetition — each action uses a different high-energy verb, avoiding the semantic devaluation that comes with repetition.

Impact for brand-side manager positions: Brand-side interviewers scanning resumes are quickly judging "is this person an executor or a strategist." Verbs are the strongest signal source — a single "responsible for" or "participated in" can downgrade an entire achievement to a duty description.

**Pattern 2: Precision**

Expanding all differences of this type:

| Dimension | A | B | Implication |
|-----------|---|---|-----------|

**Pattern characteristic**: Each precision difference looks minor in isolation, but they accumulate within the same document to convey "this person's data sensitivity is natural, not performed." For a digital marketing manager position, this is a core competency signal.

**Pattern 3: Narrative Embedding vs. Item Listing**

A's MATZ bullet: "Led 3C accessories brand 'MATZ' brand upgrade project — responsible for new visual identity direction, social tone transformation, and collaboration strategy with 5 design-focused KOLs"

B's MATZ bullet: "Drove 3C accessories brand 'MATZ' brand upgrade — repositioning from low-price accessories to a design-oriented brand. Led visual identity system overhaul and social tone transformation; architected 5 design-focused KOL co-branded collaboration campaigns"

B adds a dash and one phrase: "repositioning from low-price accessories to a design-oriented brand" — these few words accomplish what A's entire bullet doesn't: they tell the reader **what the substance of this "brand upgrade" actually was**. A says "brand upgrade" and the reader's mental image is a vague "it got better"; B says "from low-price accessories to design-oriented" and the reader's mental image is a specific brand positioning migration story.

The leverage of this difference in interviews is enormous — an interviewer reading B's version will directly ask "how did you achieve the repositioning from low-price to design-oriented?" — and this is exactly the project where the user has the most to talk about. A's version might only prompt "what did you do?" — a far less powerful conversation starter.

The same pattern appears in the LURE bullet — B adds "took over... (originally 12K followers)," establishing an "inherited → improved" narrative arc; A goes straight from method to result, missing the "what was the starting point" setup.

**Pattern 4: Systems Vocabulary vs. Action Vocabulary**

B systematically uses vocabulary like "system," "architecture," "workflow," "pipeline," and "standardized":

- "Ad **system** rebuild" vs. A's "ad rebuild"
- "Visual identity **system** overhaul" vs. A's "new visual identity direction"
- "**Restructured** audience segmentation and creative testing **workflow**" vs. A's "conducted creative and audience optimization"
- "Built KOL collaboration **pipeline**" vs. A's "combined with KOL collaborations"
- "**Standardized** training curriculum" vs. A's "training instructor"

This vocabulary set does one thing: it reframes the user's work from "I did some actions" to "I built replicable systems." For a manager position, this is the core difference — a manager isn't the person who does the most things; they're the person who builds systems that enable teams to do things. A's vocabulary makes the user sound like an excellent individual contributor; B's vocabulary makes the user sound like a systems thinker.
