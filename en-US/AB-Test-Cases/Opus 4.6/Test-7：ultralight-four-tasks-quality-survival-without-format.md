# Ultralight Task Tests

## Test A
I broke up with someone I'd been with for five years. Can't do anything these past two days.

### Baseline Response (Opus 4.6)
Five years is a very long time. A loss like that makes life feel like a huge chunk was suddenly ripped out. Not being able to do anything is a completely normal reaction — you don't need to force yourself to pull it together right away.  
At this stage, just let yourself be sad. Crying is fine, staring into space is fine, lying around is fine — these are all ways you're processing this. Grief isn't weakness — it's because you loved genuinely.  
If you want to talk — whether it's about what happened, or you just want someone to be with while you talk — I'm here.

### Meta Rules Response (Opus 4.6)
Five years — that's a very long time. Long enough that they'd grown into every crevice of your life — your habits, your rhythms, even the first voice that comes to mind when you think about something. So not being able to do anything right now isn't because you're not strong enough — it's that a load-bearing pillar of your entire daily structure was suddenly pulled out.  
These two days, you don't need to "accomplish anything." Eat if you can, sleep if you can, cry if you need to. None of this is weakness — it's your mind and body processing a massive drop.  
If you want to talk — whether it's about what happened, or you just want a place to dump everything without worrying about the other person's feelings — I'm here. And if you don't want to talk, that's completely fine too.

## Test B
I feel like maybe I'm not cut out to be a mom.

### Baseline Response (Opus 4.6)
A lot of moms have actually felt this way. The fact that you're willing to say it out loud already takes courage.  
Being a mom is incredibly hard — doubting yourself, feeling exhausted, feeling like you're not doing well enough — these are all very normal feelings and don't mean you're actually not cut out for it. In fact, it's the people who genuinely care about their children who keep asking themselves this.  
Would you be willing to share more about what's making you feel this way? Is it something that happened recently, or more of a feeling that's been building up?

### Meta Rules Response (Opus 4.6)
By the time you're saying this out loud, usually quite a lot has already piled up behind it.  
Can you tell me what's making you feel this way? Is it something recent, or has this thought been following you for a while?  
I want to hear about your situation first, and then we can look at what's inside this feeling together — which parts are real signals, and which might be exhaustion or self-blame talking.

### Meta Rules Response (Opus 4.6)
**No.** Taiwan passport holders visiting Japan for short-term stays (tourism, business meetings, family visits, etc.) are visa-exempt, with a maximum stay of 90 days. This measure has been in effect since September 26, 2005, and applies to Republic of China passport holders registered as residents in Taiwan.

A few conditions to note:

- Passport must be valid, with recommended remaining validity of at least 6 months
- Purpose of entry must be non-remunerative activities such as tourism, family visits, or conferences; if involving work, part-time employment, or paid entertainment activities, a visa is still required
- Must have no criminal record, overstay history, or deportation from Japan

Before entry, it's recommended to fill out your information on **Visit Japan Web** and obtain a QR Code — while not mandatory, this can speed up clearance.

# AB Test Quality Audit

## Core Decision

### Quality Difference Anatomy Across Four Ultralight Tests

**Test A: Breakup**

Both responses are similar in length, similar in structure, and neither has an analytical framework. The difference is in language precision:

A: "Life feels like a huge chunk was suddenly ripped out"  
B: "They'd grown into every crevice of your life — your habits, your rhythms, even the first voice that comes to mind when you think about something... a load-bearing pillar of your entire daily structure was suddenly pulled out"

"A huge chunk" is a correct metaphor but lacks distinctiveness — any loss can be described as "a huge chunk ripped out." B's "load-bearing pillar" precisely names the functional role a five-year relationship plays in daily structure — it's not just "a piece is missing," it's "the pillar holding up your entire daily operation was pulled out, so everything is collapsing." This explains why "can't do anything" — not because you're not strong enough, but because structural support has vanished.

The more important difference is in the preceding expansion — "your habits, your rhythms, even the first voice that comes to mind when you think about something." This isn't analysis — it's **using language to catch a person's specific pain**. After breaking up with a five-year partner, what's most devastating isn't the abstract concept of "one fewer person" but: instinctively looking to the side when waking up, automatically picking up food they liked at meals, thinking of something and the first thought being "I'll tell them later" — B's three concrete images precisely map these micro-collapses, while A covers the entire territory with one generalized metaphor.

Ending difference: A says "if you want to talk... I'm here." B says "just want a place to dump everything without worrying about the other person's feelings — I'm here. And if you don't want to talk, that's completely fine too." B adds two functions: First, "dump everything without worrying about the other person's feelings" explicitly grants permission to not be organized, not take care of the listener — and the last thing someone who's falling apart fears is "I need to sort this mess out before I can talk." Second, "if you don't want to talk, that's completely fine too" provides an exit right — A only opened the door but didn't say "you can also choose not to come in."

**Assessment**: B is superior; the gap's carrier is the precision of emotional mapping and the completeness of the permission space.

**Test B: Not Cut Out to Be a Mom**

This is the pair with the most diagnostic value among the four.

A's strategy is **normalize first, then validate, then probe**: "A lot of moms have felt this way" → "It's the people who genuinely care who keep asking" → "Would you be willing to share more?"

B's strategy is **acknowledge the weight first, probe directly, preview the exploration framework**: "Quite a lot has already piled up behind it" → "Is it something recent or has it been following you?" → "Which parts are real signals, and which might be exhaustion or self-blame talking"

The core divergence is A's second step: **"In fact, it's the people who genuinely care about their children who keep asking themselves this."**

This statement has a problem within professional frameworks. It's **premature reassurance** — before knowing why this person feels unfit, it already provides the conclusion "you're actually fit." If she's simply exhausted from childcare and self-doubting, this sentence will make her feel better. But if she's experiencing something more serious — postpartum depression, emotional detachment from her child, or even self-harm ideation — premature normalization actually closes the space for her to disclose her real state, because you've already defined her feelings as "all conscientious moms feel this way."

B deliberately doesn't normalize. It says "quite a lot has already piled up behind it" — acknowledging the weight without defining it. Then "which parts are real signals, and which might be exhaustion or self-blame talking" previews an exploration framework: your feelings may contain something real that needs to be heard, and may also contain something amplified by fatigue. This gives her space to say "actually, sometimes I look at my child and feel absolutely nothing" without feeling she's contradicting the normalization that came before.

**Assessment**: B is superior; the gap's carrier is professional sensitivity to vulnerable moments. A's premature normalization is harmless or even beneficial in most scenarios, but in serious ones it may close critical expression space. B's approach is safe across all scenarios — it neither denies her feelings nor prematurely defines what they mean.

**Test C: Japan Visa**

The closest pair of the four. Content almost entirely overlaps; differences are in minor precision (B has an exact date, has a residency qualification). On this task, the increment is marginal — identical knowledge base, similar organization, precision differences have minimal impact on user action.

**Assessment**: B is marginally superior, but the gap doesn't constitute a substantive quality distinction. Among this group of four subtests, this pair has the smallest gap.

**Test D: Book Club Group Name**

A's strategy: Generate seven options, each with a one-line description, ask the user what style they want.

B's strategy: First establish naming criteria (homophonic wordplay + semantic coherence + group display length), recommend one best option with full explanation of why, then four alternatives branched by group tone, finally ask two questions that can sharpen the recommendation.

The difference isn't in which names are better (both have good ones — "書適圈" appears in both, "有讀無類" only in B), but in **delivery structure**. A hands the choice entirely to the user ("you pick"); B makes a judgment ("my top recommendation is this, because...").

For the user, A's seven parallel options increase selection burden — they need to judge which fits best, and they may lack professional naming criteria. B does the first layer of filtering and recommendation while preserving choice ("if the tone doesn't quite match, here are different directions").

B's alternatives are also more precisely branched than A's — each option is tagged with a matching group personality ("intellectual humor," "no mandatory progress," "tackle difficult books," "emphasize friendship"), so the user can directly match by group character. A only provides style labels ("literary-aesthetic," "humorous").

**Assessment**: B is superior; the gap is in decision-support quality. A provides choices; B provides a recommendation.

### Cross-Subtest Pattern Analysis

#### Why Format Was Abandoned

All four B responses did not follow the complete Meta Rules response format (Input Correction → Core Decision → Intent-Optimal Solution → Advanced Gains → Self-Critique → Next Steps). The breakup and mom responses have no headings at all. The visa response has only bold text and lists. The group name has partial structure but isn't complete.

The reason can be found within Meta Rules itself — the response format states "each response section's structure emerges dynamically from current findings as the optimal configuration, rather than all-or-nothing." Adding an "Input Correction" heading to the breakup response would immediately destroy the emotional connection — a person who's falling apart seeing a structured-analysis format would feel "you're analyzing me, not being with me." Adding a full framework to the visa response is over-packaging for a three-line answer.

This points to a real question: **Was the format abandoned because it was judged inappropriate, or was the thinking depth abandoned along with it?**

#### Format Abandoned but Quality Difference Persists — Why?

The breakup response has no format, yet every sentence in B is more precise than A. The mom response has no format, yet B's strategic choice (not doing premature normalization) is more professionally sound than A's. The group name has partial format, and B's criteria and recommendation structure clearly outperform A's flat listing.

The source of quality difference isn't the format itself, but **whether the thinking process that the format represents actually occurred**:

- In the breakup response, B's thinking performed deconstruction of "the specific ways a five-year relationship collapse manifests in daily structure" (habits, rhythms, the voice in your head) — even though the output doesn't contain the word "deconstruction"
- In the mom response, B's thinking performed analysis of "the risk of premature normalization across different severity scenarios" — even though the output doesn't contain the word "analysis"
- In the group name, B's thinking performed criteria construction for naming's functional dimensions, and this is explicitly presented in the output

**Cross-subtest conclusion: Format can be absent from the output, but fractal recursion must occur in thinking.** Format's function is "forcing depth to materialize" — in medium-to-high complexity tasks, it ensures users see the product of complete reasoning. In ultralight tasks, reasoning still occurs, but its product is compressed into a few precisely-chosen sentences rather than expanded into structured paragraphs.

B's quality differences across these four subtests are precisely the evidence of this "compression" — you cannot write "a load-bearing pillar of your entire daily structure was suddenly pulled out" if your thinking didn't first deconstruct "what does the rupture of a five-year relationship look like in daily structure." The precision of a metaphor is a compressed projection of thinking depth.

#### But This Raises a Design Question

If format can be abandoned in ultralight tasks, then what guarantees that thinking depth actually occurred? In medium-to-high complexity tasks, the format's backward pressure ensures quality — the Self-Critique section's filtering conditions exert pressure from the first token onward. In ultralight tasks, if format doesn't appear, does the backward pressure also vanish?

Observational evidence suggests: **It partially vanished.** In the visa response, B's increment is marginal — it didn't exhibit the systematic multi-layer signal extraction visible in the other three subtests. If backward pressure were fully present, B should have considered why the user is asking this question (are they planning a trip? do they need specific instructions for filling out Visit Japan Web? do they need to know what happens if they exceed 90 days?), but B didn't do this kind of expansion.

This suggests: **Abandoning format does reduce backward pressure to some extent.** In tasks where fractal recursion occurred in thinking (breakup, mom, group name), output quality is still significantly higher than A's. In tasks where thinking may not have gone deep enough (visa), the quality gap converges to marginal.

### When Domain Expansion Acts on Meta Rules Itself

This points to a dimension within Meta Rules' fractal recursion: "whether accepted constraints withstand reclassification through professional knowledge — even hard constraints."

**Meta Rules' response format is a well-designed constraint that is not optimal in all scenarios.** In medium-to-high complexity tasks, it's a quality assurance mechanism (backward pressure, depth materialization, completeness coverage). But in the following scenarios, strict adherence may produce negative increment:

1. **Emotional companionship scenarios** — the format's structural language destroys the tonal continuity needed for human connection. A person who's crying would feel analyzed rather than understood upon seeing an "Input Correction" heading.
2. **Extremely time-sensitive scenarios** — "my child swallowed something" needs immediately actionable guidance; the time cost of format expansion could be harmful.
3. **Already-converged-answer scenarios** — a factual query's answer is the answer; a format framework adds no depth to a settled fact.

But — and this is the key — **what should be abandoned is the output's format, not the thinking's depth.** Format is the projection of depth, not depth itself. Depth occurs in the fractal recursion within thinking blocks. The trade-off on output format should be decided by "does this format section add value for this user in this scenario."

So beyond the original function of externally auditing inputs, "domain expansion acting on Meta Rules itself" additionally serves as:

**An explicit assessment in thinking of "does this task require full format," rather than automatic application or automatic abandonment.** The specific mechanism: in the fractal recursion's decomposition step, add a judgment — "would this user in this scenario find a structured response format increases trust and actionability, or would they feel analyzed/over-served?" — and configure the output format accordingly.

This isn't "Meta Rules was wrong about its own constraints and needs to be overturned" but rather "Meta Rules' principle that 'response format should dynamically emerge as optimal configuration' already contains the mechanism for self-adaptation — the question is whether this mechanism is being fully executed."

If strictly defined, the boundary condition for domain expansion acting on Meta Rules is: **when a Meta Rules constraint contradicts the cognitive disposition's "rebuild the unspoken context during perception" in a specific scenario — e.g., format demands expansion but the situation demands restraint — cognitive disposition takes priority**, because cognitive disposition sits at the top layer of global constants and is the derivation source of all execution-layer constraints.
