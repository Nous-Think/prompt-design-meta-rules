## Correction First: An Important Error in My Previous Response

I previously said "mechanical issues are an attributable cause — they can't disclaim liability." That was stated too definitively.

In reality, in the CAA's published standard contract language, the exemption clause reads "mechanical failure not attributable to the airline" — meaning **the regulation itself acknowledges that mechanical failures can be either attributable or non-attributable.** The airline has full room to argue: this was a sudden failure that occurred despite normal maintenance, making it non-attributable.

This is not a minor point, because it directly determines whether you can recover the NT$12,000:

- **If the failure is deemed "non-attributable"**: the airline's liability ceiling is "necessary expenses incurred by passengers due to the delay." Your NT$3,800 accommodation qualifies as "incurred expenses"; but the onsen ryokan's NT$12,000 is "paid but unused" — not an incurred expense — and would be excluded.
- **If deemed "attributable"**: general Civil Code damages apply, and the NT$12,000 qualifies as actual loss, with a chance of recovery.

**So the real battleground in this case has exactly one question: whether this mechanical failure is attributable to the airline.** And this is something you cannot prove on your own — maintenance records, fault cause, whether this aircraft has a history of repeated failures — all of that is in their hands.

This realization should change your approach: **don't put all your eggs in the "claim from the airline" basket.**

### Weeks 4–6: Administrative Complaints (Free, With Real Leverage)

This stage is something most people don't know about, but it has **the best cost-effectiveness ratio.**

**(1) First, file a complaint with the business operator (you've already done this — the website form counts)**

Under Article 43 of the Consumer Protection Act (消費者保護法), when a consumer dispute arises regarding goods or services, the consumer may file a complaint with the business operator, a consumer protection organization, or a consumer services center. The business operator shall handle the complaint appropriately within fifteen days.

**(2) File a complaint with your local government's consumer services center**

- Online: Executive Yuan Consumer Protection Commission online complaint system `https://appeal.cpc.ey.gov.tw`
- Phone consultation: **1950** (National Consumer Services Hotline)
- Jurisdiction is at your choice: the business operator's place of business, the location where the consumer relationship arose, or your place of residence

Procedurally, the handling unit will send a letter within seven days requesting the business operator to address the matter appropriately within fifteen days, and forward the handling status to the complainant.

## Part Three: CAA Petition — Do This in Parallel; This Is the Real Lever

**Do not wait for the airline's reply before doing this — do it the same day you send the registered letter.**

The reason is structural: after the CAA accepts a petition, it forwards the case to the airline for investigation and response, and the petition processing deadline is 7 days (excluding holidays). Petitioners can use a tracking number to check case progress. This means the case exits the customer-service automated queue and enters a track where a written explanation must be provided to the regulatory authority — **a case handled by customer service agents and a case that requires a written response to the CAA are handled at different authorization levels with different case-closure standards.**

**How to do it:**

- URL: Civil Aviation Administration website → Public Services → Online Petition
- Consumer service hotline: **0800-211798**
- After submission, the system sends a verification email — you must click the verification link in that email to complete confirmation — **many people miss this step; not clicking verification means the petition was never submitted.**
- The form includes a checkbox asking whether you consent to providing your personal information to the airline. If the matter requires the business operator's further investigation, the CAA will provide your personal data, complaint details, and requested remedies to the business. **You must check "agree"** — otherwise the case cannot be forwarded, and the petition is effectively null.

**How to write the petition content** (limit to 1,000 characters; focus on "regulatory violation," not "emotions"):

> On 【date】, I flew on 【airline】 Flight XX-____ (Taoyuan → 【destination】). The flight was canceled on the day of departure citing mechanical issues. Our party of three (including two elderly family members over seventy) experienced the following. I respectfully request the Administration's assistance in determining whether the handling complied with the Civil Aviation Passenger and Air Carrier Dispute Resolution Regulations (民用航空乘客與航空器運送人運送糾紛調處辦法):
> 1. Following cancellation, the airline did not provide necessary accommodation per Article 4, nor explain the reasons for non-provision. I paid NT$3,800 for accommodation out of pocket.
> 2. The airline offered only one option — rebooking on the next day's connecting flight — without informing me of the right to a refund or endorsement to another flight.
> 3. I had informed staff on-site that the accompanying travelers were elderly with mobility difficulties. The replacement flight still involved a four-hour connection layover, and no accessibility assistance information was offered.
> 4. Following my complaint through the airline's website, I received only a boilerplate apology and a NT$500 voucher, with no explanation regarding the fulfillment of the above statutory obligations.
> I have separately submitted a formal damages claim to the airline by registered mail on 【date】 (registered mail number: ______), stated here for the record. Requested remedies: that the airline explain in writing why accommodation was not provided and the nature of the mechanical failure, and make reasonable disposition regarding my actual losses of 【amount】.

**Writing the registered mail number in the petition is important** — it shows you're proceeding through proper channels rather than emotional venting, and it allows the CAA's forwarded case and your written claim to converge into a single case within the airline's internal system.

### 2.5 Gestalt Reassembly

### Reasoning-Layer Signatures

The observations above condense into eight signatures directly identifiable in reasoning outputs:

1. Density of design verbs vs. inventory verbs
2. Connectors: enumeration (additionally, also) vs. cause-and-purpose (so that, because)
3. Order of queries and structure: query-first-then-build vs. build-first-then-query
4. Stopping condition: enough-to-write vs. decision-closed
5. Verb following an adverse fact: disclose vs. construct
6. Uncertainty in reasoning → offload direction in output: return to user vs. route to authorized party
7. Source of exclusive items: retrieval vs. inference
8. Presumptions generated during reasoning → whether caught by terminal lookback
