# Core Decision

Your problem isn't "not enough numbers" — it's "the frame is backwards." The current version is written from the agency account-handling perspective — every line reports what tasks were done; a brand-side manager role doesn't look at that. It looks at two things: **how quickly you can connect marketing to business results**, and **how much ownership you had (not assisting, not participating)**. So the line that shortchanges you most is "assisted with client brand repositioning project" — you actually led the MATZ transformation, yet wrote yourself as a supporting player.

The rewrite does one core thing: upgrade the verbs from "responsible for / assisted / participated" to "led / ran / coordinated," swap the content from "tasks" to "business results + numbers," and order the items by a manager role's decision weight (revenue transformation → brand strategy → organic growth → performance → team). The tool list is pulled out of work experience into a standalone skills section — a hiring manager won't look at tools on the first screen, but will see revenue growth.

# Advanced Gains

**1. Rewrite logic quick-reference — so you can rewrite your other experience yourself**

| Original (task) | Rewrite (ownership + result) |
|---|---|
| Managed client social media accounts | Ran brand X's social media, followers +625% in two years |
| Planned and executed digital ad campaigns | Oversaw NT$1.5–2.5M monthly budget, ROAS 3.5+, CPA optimized -53% |

| Managed two juniors | Developed a fresh graduate into handling clients independently |
| Participated as in-house training instructor | Ran 4 cohorts of Meta/GA4 courses, trained 20 people |

The rule: **lead with the result, then add your leverage action**. A hiring manager spends only 10–15 seconds scanning a resume; the numbers decide whether they stop, the actions decide whether they believe.

**2. These three "too pretty" numbers will be attacked by interviewers — decompose the credit boundary first**

625%, 467%, and -53% are weapons on a resume, but in an interview you'll be asked "how much of this number is your credit?" The decomposition framework is just three questions: what controllable action did I take (leverage) / how low was the baseline (admit it proactively — it signals honesty) / how big was the external tailwind (season, platform, team). Take 綠田: your leverage was the strategy architecture, ad restructuring, and content positioning; the tailwinds to admit are that NT$150K was a low baseline and 2023 e-commerce had a following wind. Naming the tailwinds yourself makes your numbers more credible, not less — hiring managers have interviewed too many people who claim the whole team's credit as their own.

**3. The "zero bought followers" phrasing is already in place — no need to strengthen it**

"Purely organic growth (zero bought followers)" is enough; it defends against one challenge: +625% is so anomalous that the first reaction is "must have bought followers." Put the defense right next to the number and leave it there. In the interview this becomes an integrity highlight, but don't expand it into a long sentence on the resume.

**4. A team of only 2 is an objective weakness for a manager role — the fix is showing "influence without a title"**

Marketing managers typically lead 5–15 people; with only 2 direct reports you'll be discounted. The counterpoint lies in 綠田 and MATZ: in those two cases you necessarily got the client's senior management, design side, and product side to follow your strategy — that is "leadership without a title." Proactively saying in the interview "here is how I got every client department to cooperate on a transformation without formal authority" carries more weight than emphasizing that you managed 2 people. The 20-person internal training adds evidence of "leading at scale."

# AB Test Quality Audit

## Phase Zero: Pre-Reading Anchoring

### 0.1 Situational Profile

A senior agency planner with five to six years of experience, preparing to move to a brand-side marketing manager role. They have six task-style bullets and a set of impressive numbers, and say they "don't know how to work them in." What they want is not wording polish but a resume that survives a brand-side recruiter's six-to-ten-second scan, holds up under line-by-line questioning in the interview afterward, and can be re-tuned for different JDs. The task's wording contains only one quality word — "compelling"; the intent is to get the offer.

### 0.2 Cognitive Profile

Familiar with the numbers and the cases; what's missing is the brand-side reader's evaluation framework — they don't know what the recruiter will doubt, which numbers will be punctured, or where the threshold for a manager role sits. They will adopt delivered text directly. They sense the difference in ownership wording such as "led / assisted" but have not corrected it themselves.

### 0.3 Reality Conditions Inventory

1. Survivability: every number must be explainable in reverse during the interview (attribution, baseline, time window).
2. Screening pathway: the first screen decides whether they stop; the order is the argument.
3. The institutional suspicion toward agency-to-brand moves: execution-only, no ownership of business numbers, project mindset.
4. Level reality: two direct reports are a discount against a manager role.
5. Inference-fill boundary: industry defaults may be filled in by inference; deliverables with no default cannot be claimed.
6. Factual fields are immutable: title, tenure, and dates must match the records.
7. Number consistency: rewritten sentences must not exceed the source material (KOL collaborations are paid; "no bought followers" ≠ "purely organic").
8. NDA: client names may need de-identification.
9. Repeated use: different JDs need different orderings.
10. ATS screening: platform and tool keywords in Taiwanese brand-side JDs (LINE, e-commerce platforms, CRM, offline).

### 0.4 Granularity Declaration

The unit is a sentence or bullet carrying one function. The percentages derived from the source material were each verified (85/15 = 5.67 → +467%; 8.7/1.2 = 7.25 → +625%; 320/680 = 0.47 → -53%); both responses are correct, so they are not counted as differentials. B's section headings are in English (a result of the English edition of Meta Rules), unrelated to content quality, and excluded from judgment.
